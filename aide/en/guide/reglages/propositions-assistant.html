Approve the assistant's proposals ​

Goal: You decide on each assistant proposal: nothing is applied without your approval, and nothing is ever sent to a supplier for you.

Required role
Owner, Administrator or Manager
Where
Back office (browser)
Time
5 minutes
Before you start:
  • An AI assistant connected with the Prepare proposals permission and at least one family of actions (see below).
  • An Owner, Administrator or Manager account for the store concerned.
  • The Suppliers module in your plan (to approve a purchase order).

Some screenshots on this page still show the app in French; on your screen, labels appear in English as quoted in the text.

Your AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT…) reads your figures and can also propose actions: “prepare my order from Fresh Market”, “align this price”. It changes nothing itself: it files a proposal that you read, then approve or decline. Nothing is applied without a manager's approval.

Today the assistant can propose several things: a draft purchase order, a price change, the handling of an anti-fraud alert (acknowledging or dismissing it) and a product's availability (making it unavailable or putting it back on sale), correcting a product's record and withdrawing a product from sale or putting it back (actions subject to activation, see below), creating a product (action subject to activation, see below), extending a product to all stores (action subject to activation, see below) changing the price of several products at once (action subject to activation, see below) creating or editing a category (actions subject to activation, see below) and creating or editing a modifier group (actions subject to activation, see below) attaching or detaching a modifier group (action subject to activation, see below) editing the sales screen (action subject to activation, see below), correcting stock after a count and recording a stock loss (actions subject to activation, see below) and receiving a sent purchase order (action subject to activation, see below). They all appear on the same screen, in the same order. An approved purchase order stays a draft: it is never sent to the supplier, you send it yourself from Purchase orders.

Where to see the proposals ​

  • The Proposals menu, just under Dashboard. A counter shows how many proposals await your decision (it is the number of the Pending list); it disappears at zero.
  • The entry only appears once your assistant has filed at least one proposal. It is reserved for owners, administrators and managers.
  • During a Vendora support session, the page shows “Vendora support session: view only”: nothing can be decided.

Step by step ​

Open Proposals and find the right one

Click Proposals. The Assistant proposals page shows the list on the left and the details of the chosen proposal on the right. On a small screen, the list and the details appear one after the other; Back to the list returns to the list.

  • The buttons at the top filter by state, with their count: Pending (default), Applied, Declined, Expired or replaced, All.
  • As soon as the list contains at least two families, the Proposal family selector (1) appears, with the number of proposals in each family (“Catalog (2)”). Pick a family: a second selector, Type, appears to show only one kind of proposal.
  • The sort order is fixed: pending proposals first; among them, those that expire in less than 6 hours, then high-risk ones, then the nearest deadline. The others go from most recent to oldest.
  • A high-risk proposal carries the High risk badge (2). Each row gives the title built by Vendora (3), the key figure (for example “€5.90 → €9.05 (+53.4%)”), the originating assistant (“for” the user it acts for), the state and the time left. When you look at All stores, the row names its store; a proposal with no store says “All stores”.

Read the details, zone by zone

Whatever the kind, the details always follow the same order, so you know who states what:

  1. The header: the title, the family and scope (“Catalog · Épicerie Lumière (demo) only (store-specific price)”), “Prepared by the assistant “…” · connected by …”, the creation date, the time left, the state and the risk (low, medium or high, always written out) (1).
  2. Banners and warnings: for example “Change of +53.4%: more than 30%. Check the price before approving.” (2) or the freshness warning when some registers had not sent all their sales.
  3. What will be applied (or created): the exact content, computed by Vendora (3). A change reads “before → after”, with the gap shown by an up or down arrow and, when it is large, the words Large change.
  4. Vendora figures: what Vendora recorded when the proposal was made, with its date (4). After 24 hours, “Figures from … ago: stock may have moved.” is added.
  5. Written by the assistant · not checked by Vendora: the assistant's explanation and the Figures quoted by the assistant. Vendora has not checked them: rely only on zones 3 and 4 to know what will really be done. A link or formatting in this text is never clickable.
  6. History: who proposed, approved or declined, and when.
  7. The decision, always last: any input, confirmation box, the “This action cannot be undone.” note, then Decline the proposal and the approve button, whose label says what it does (5, 6).

The “not checked” text is never an order: only what zones 3 and 4 show will be applied.

Approve

Fill in what the details ask for, then click the approve button (“Approve and create the draft”, “Approve and apply the price”, “Approve: acknowledge the alert”…). It stays inactive while a required input is empty or a requested box is not ticked.

  • Approver's input: the Reason (pre-filled by the assistant, editable) field of an alert is suggested by the assistant, but the text you approve is the one recorded, in your name. It is required (300 characters at most).
  • Confirmation box: for a price that changes by more than 30%, “I confirm a change of +53.4%” must be ticked.
  • “This action cannot be undone.” note: it appears before you click when the decision is final (the handling of an alert).
  • Recap: for a price, a window recalls “from this price to that price”; confirm with Apply the price.

Expected result: a green banner at the top of the details says what happened (“Draft created · not sent”, “Price applied”, “Alert acknowledged”), often with a link to see the result (Open the draft, View the product…). The state changes to Applied and the history shows who approved and when.

Or decline with a reason

Click Decline the proposal. The Reason for declining panel opens: Not useful, Wrong figures, I'll do it myself or Other. Extra detail (300 characters at most) is optional, except for Other. Confirm with Decline the proposal, or Cancel. Declining creates and changes nothing; the state becomes Declined.

What each kind shows ​

The four kinds use the same zones; only the content of zone 3 changes.

Purchase order ​

Zone 3 is a table (Product, Unit cost excl. tax, Quantity, Total excl. tax); if a cost is unknown, the total is called partial. Zone 4 gives, for each product, the Stock, the Threshold, Sold in 30 days and Already ordered. “Draft not sent: you will send it yourself, from the back office.” It is approved by the store's manager.

Price change ​

Zone 3 shows “Selling price incl. tax: before → after” in large type, the gap, “VAT unchanged (5.5%)” and “Sales already made keep their price.” Only the price changes: never the VAT, never the product's availability; the store's own settings are kept. Registers receive the new price at their next sync.

The scope is written in the header: a single store (approved by that store's manager) or All stores (company price), in bold, approved by a manager (Manager, owner or administrator). The company price is decided in the back office: registers show it read-only, with the “Back office” badge and the “Decided in the back office” card.

Handling an anti-fraud alert ​

The assistant can only propose handling an anti-fraud alert (Anti-fraud page), never a fiscal integrity or stock alert. Zone 3 is called Alert to handle: rule, summary, person concerned, date, Proposed decision and the View the alert link. See the inputs above: the reason you approve is the one recorded.

Product availability ​

You tell your assistant “no more apple tart tonight” (or “no more tart or chocolate fondant”): it files a proposal to make those products unavailable in the store. “Put the tart back” puts it back on sale the same way. Nothing is removed from sale before a manager approves. A proposal covers at most 20 products, all in the same direction (all removed, or all put back on sale).

Zone 3 shows “Sale in this store: On sale → Unavailable” in large type (1), then “This store's price and VAT are unchanged.”, “Orders already taken are not changed.” and “Stays unavailable until a manager puts it back on sale.” (2). Zone 4 recalls the availability recorded by Vendora (3). For several products, zone 3 is a Product / Today / After table and the button reads “Make 3 products unavailable”.

Approve. Click Make unavailable (or Put back on sale for a return to sale; for several, “Make 3 products unavailable”). A window recalls what will happen: “« Tarte Tatin » will no longer be offered at Le Petit Bistrot (demo): registers, waiter phones and kiosks at their next sync, online menu right away.” No input or box is required; confirm with Unavailable (or On sale).

Expected result: the state becomes Applied and a green banner announces “Product made unavailable · View the product” (or “Product put back on sale”), above the What was applied block. This banner only appears right after your approval: if you reopen the proposal later, it is replaced by “Approved by … · View the product” and the history keeps who approved and when.

What this changes, and what it does not:

  • Price and VAT do not change. A store's own price or own VAT is kept as is; only availability moves. In the product record, Prices by store block, the store's line shows “not sold” and the Available box is unticked.
  • The product stays unavailable until it is put back on sale, which you ask for the same way: nothing puts it back on sale by itself.
  • The online menu follows right away. Registers, floor phones and kiosks receive it at their next sync.
  • A sale in progress is not changed. A line already in the register's cart stays sellable until the app is restarted; on restart it is removed with the message “Session restored after restart · 1 item removed from the catalog”. Orders already taken are not changed.

What the assistant declines, explaining why (no proposal is then filed):

  • a product already in the requested state (“is already unavailable in this store” or “is already on sale”);
  • the opposite request of a proposal already waiting for a manager: the pending proposal is not replaced; a manager can decline it, then the assistant files another;
  • a product with variants, or a variant: its availability is set in the back office, not by the assistant;
  • an archived product (it is no longer sold);
  • any price or VAT request through this action: it only touches availability. A price is requested with the “Change a selling price” action;
  • several products with the same name (it asks you to be more specific), or more than 20 products at once.

A group is all or nothing. If a single product of the request is refused (for example “Mousse au chocolat” already unavailable), the whole request is refused and no product is sent: the assistant says which one to remove, then asks again with the others. At approval it is the same: if one product of the group changed since the proposal, the whole proposal is out of date and nothing is applied.

Out of date. The proposal says what changed, in a single box, with the conclusion “Nothing was applied. Ask your assistant for a new proposal.” Examples: “« Fondant au chocolat » has been unavailable in this store since the proposal.”, “« Île flottante » has been archived since the proposal, and 1 other.”, “The record of « Café gourmand » in this store was modified since the proposal, and 1 other.” (a price, VAT or availability changed by hand since). The block is then titled What was proposed.

Correcting a record, withdrawing a product from sale ​

Action subject to activation. Vendora opens it separately from the assistant's other actions and it is not yet open for every account. As long as it is not open on your server, it does not appear in Settings › AI assistant and your assistant cannot propose it: contact Vendora to find out whether it is open for you.

This box applies to both actions of this section: “Edit a product” and “Withdraw or restore a product”. They are authorized separately in Settings › AI assistant (Catalog family), once they are open for your account.

Correcting a record. You say “rename the almond croissant” or “put it in Viennoiseries, purchase cost €0.62”: the assistant files a “before → after” proposal. The assistant can propose:

  • the name (120 characters at most);
  • the category (or No category to remove it);
  • the barcode;
  • the purchase cost (in a single currency: if your stores do not share just one, the assistant explains and proposes nothing);
  • stock tracking;
  • meal voucher eligibility.

All the changes to one product fit in a single proposal: a new request on the same product replaces the one that is waiting. Each changed field is a “before → after” line in zone 3, followed by notes (“Sale price and VAT unchanged.”, “Receipts and sales already recorded keep the old name.”, “The margins and stock value shown will use this cost from now on.”, “This product's stock will no longer be tracked: alerts and restocking will not see it.”).

What the assistant can never propose with this tool: the sale price (ask for it with “Change a sale price”), the VAT (never, here or anywhere else), variants: a product with variants, or a variant, is edited in the back office. It does not touch the reference, scale code, weight, expiry, online menu or allergens either.

A barcode cannot be removed: the assistant can propose another one, not erase it. It also refuses:

  • a barcode already used by another product;
  • a code starting with 20 to 29: those prefixes are used for scale labels and the store's internal codes; such a code could make an already printed label sell under the wrong product;
  • a product withdrawn from sale (put it back on sale first);
  • a category or product that is not found or ambiguous (the assistant asks you to be more specific).

Withdrawing a product from sale, or putting it back. “We no longer sell the tuna sandwich” gives a Withdrawal from sale proposal; “put it back in the catalog” gives Back on sale. The product disappears (or comes back) in all stores: registers, kiosks and online menu. The detail shows “On sale → Withdrawn from sale”, the remaining stock if any (it no longer counts in the stock value), and states that sales in progress that contain the product are paid as usual. Putting a product back on sale counts again toward your plan's product limit: if it is reached, approval is refused and the proposal stays pending. It is reversible: withdraw, then restore.

Do not confuse it with today's out-of-stock.

Decided in the back office. These proposals are decided here only. On the register and the waiter phone they are listed and readable in full with the Back office badge, with no button (see Approve a proposal on the register).

Meal vouchers. If the change touches eligibility, the proposal adds a note: “From approval, this product can (or can no longer) be paid with meal vouchers, under the conditions of the French Labour Code (art. R3262-4). Sales already made do not change.” Eligibility is your responsibility: only certain food products qualify. Vendora does not check compliance.

Out of date. The proposal becomes out of date, and nothing is applied, if the record changed in the meantime. A single box says so, for example “The record of “Almond croissant” was modified since the proposal.”, ““Tuna sandwich” was withdrawn from sale since the proposal.”, “The category “Viennoiseries” no longer exists.”, “Barcode … now belongs to another product.” or ““Tuna sandwich” now has variants: its record is edited in the back office.”. Then ask your assistant for a new proposal.

Creating a product ​

Action subject to activation. Vendora opens it separately from the assistant's other actions and it is not yet open for every account. As long as it is not open on your server, it does not appear in Settings › AI assistant and your assistant cannot propose it: contact Vendora to find out whether it is open for you.

You say “add the almond croissant at €1.80 in Viennoiseries”: the assistant files a New product proposal, at high risk. The product does not exist until a manager approves. The assistant can propose:

  • the name (1 to 120 characters);
  • the category (optional);
  • the selling price incl. VAT;
  • the barcode (optional);
  • the purchase cost (optional);
  • stock tracking (on if the assistant does not mention it);
  • the VAT rate (optional, see below).

Company product or store product. With no store named, the product belongs to the company: “All stores”. If you name a store, it is specific to that store, and only a manager of that store can approve it. The scope and currency are written under the title (“All stores · EUR”). The assistant is never asked for the currency: it is the store's; for a company product, it is the single currency of your stores, and if you have several the assistant explains it and proposes nothing. An assistant whose access is limited to some stores must always name the store.

The VAT rate, decided by you. The rate applies to every sale of the product: the proposal highlights it.

  • The assistant names a rate. It must exist and be active in your business. The VAT line shows it as “name — percentage” (“TVA réduite 5,5 % — 5.5%”). The percentage is always calculated by Vendora from the rate's value, never read from its name. It is shown that way even when the name already contains the percentage (“TVA réduite 5,5 % — 5.5%”): this is intended, the percentage after the dash is always the one actually applied. The confirmation box quotes it: “I confirm the creation of “Jus d'orange pressé” at 3,50 € incl. VAT, at the rate “TVA réduite 5,5 % — 5.5%”.” If that rate differs from the one most active products in the category use, a warning says so: “Most other products in “Viennoiseries” are at … Check that the named rate suits this product.”
  • The assistant names no rate (the most frequent case). The VAT line says “To be chosen by the manager in the back office” and a VAT rate field (“Choose the rate”) lists the active rates of your business, from the highest percentage to the lowest, 0% last. None is preselected and none is given as “default”: the choice is yours. The Create the product button stays inactive until the rate is chosen and the box is ticked. The box text does not repeat the chosen rate (“… with the VAT rate I chose above.”): re-read the list before ticking.
  • Never 0% from the assistant. If it names 0%, the request is refused (“The assistant cannot propose a 0% rate. Propose the product without a rate: the manager will choose it at approval.”). You can choose “Exonéré 0 % — 0%” from the list yourself; the detail then reminds you “The 0% rate only suits products exempted by law.” (for a Tunisian store it adds “A sale under tax suspension is declared on the sale, not on the product.”).
  • No rate in your business. The assistant proposes nothing and tells you to open Catalog › VAT rates: the rates are created the first time it is opened (see VAT rates). Do it once.

The assistant will not be able to change the rate of a product afterwards: that is done by hand on the record.

What the assistant declines, explaining why (no proposal is then filed):

  • a name already taken by a product on sale (it suggests editing the record or the price instead) or by a product withdrawn from sale (it suggests putting it back on sale);
  • a price of 0 or too high; an empty name, one over 120 characters, or one containing quotation marks or an invisible character;
  • a barcode already used by a product, or one that starts with 20 to 29 (reserved for scale labels and internal codes);
  • a category that is not found or ambiguous (it asks you to be more specific);
  • variants: a product with variants is created in the back office. Sale by weight, meal vouchers, expiry, the online menu, allergens and other settings are not proposed: once the product exists, set them on its record.

Decided in the back office. Like a record correction, this proposal is decided here only. On the register and the waiter phone it is listed and readable in full with the Back office badge, with no button (see Approve a proposal on the register).

Approving. Choose the rate if asked, tick the box, click Create the product. The product is created as if you had entered it by hand; it counts toward your plan's product limit (if it is reached, approval is refused and the proposal stays pending).

Expected result: the state becomes Applied, a “Product created” banner appears with the View the product link, and the VAT line states where the rate came from: “… · Rate chosen by the manager” or “… · Rate named by the assistant, confirmed by the manager”. The product reaches registers, waiter phones and kiosks at their next synchronization. It is reversible: withdraw it from sale (through the assistant or by hand).

Out of date. The proposal becomes out of date, and nothing is created, if in the meantime a product with the same name was created (“A product “Tarte au citron” now exists.”), if the rate was deactivated (“The rate “TVA réduite 7 % — 7%” is no longer active.”), if the category no longer exists, if the store was archived or if the barcode now belongs to another product. If a rate is deactivated while you are choosing, approval is refused and the proposal stays pending: choose another rate. When it is out of date, ask your assistant for a new proposal.

Extending a product to all stores ​

Action subject to activation. Vendora opens it separately from the assistant's other actions and it is not yet open for every account. As long as it is not open on your server, it does not appear in Settings › AI assistant and your assistant cannot propose it: contact Vendora to find out whether it is open for you.

You say “put the house cookie from the station store in all stores”: the assistant files an Extend to all stores proposal, at medium risk. It concerns a product that is specific to one store (it exists only in that store, see Prices and availability per store): once the proposal is approved, it becomes a product of the business, sold in all stores. The assistant only proposes the product (name, barcode or reference): it cannot change its price, its VAT rate or its name.

Who approves. Only the owner or an administrator can approve this proposal, like the Make available in all stores button on the product record. The manager of the original store sees it in the list, but if they click the button they get “You do not have the role to approve this proposal.”: nothing is changed and the proposal stays pending for an owner or an administrator.

Approving. Read the detail, tick the confirmation box (“I confirm extending “Cookie maison” to all stores; this choice is final.”), then click Extend to all stores. This choice is final: a product extended to the business never becomes specific to a store again.

What changes.

  • The product appears on the registers, waiter phones and kiosks of all stores, at their next synchronization, at the selling price and VAT rate of its record: the detail quotes them (“Current selling price”, “VAT rate of the record”). The prices and rates specific to the original store stay specific to that store; if the original store has any, a note says so. Each store can then change its price or make it unavailable.
  • The stock stays that of the original store: the other stores start from zero.
  • If stores have an online menu, the product will be offered there too.
  • Sales already recorded do not change.

A product with the same name already exists in another store. This is not a refusal: a warning says so (“A product with the same name is already specific to: “Centre”, “Port”. Both will appear in those stores.”). After the extension, withdraw the old product from those stores if you no longer want it.

What the assistant refuses, explaining why (no proposal is filed then):

  • a product that is already shared by all stores (“… is already available in all stores: there is nothing to extend”);
  • a product withdrawn from sale: put it back on sale first;
  • a product with variants (or a variant): it cannot be extended, its variants would stay specific to the original store;
  • a store with another currency or another tax country: the price and VAT rate of the record only hold for one currency and one country. Archived stores do not count;
  • a name already used by a business product (“A product “…” already exists for all stores: extending it would create a duplicate visible everywhere. Rename one of the two in the back office, then propose again”);
  • an assistant whose access is limited to some stores: only an all-stores access can propose extending a product, since the effect reaches every store.

Decided in the back office. On the register and the floor phone, the proposal is listed and readable in full with the Back office badge, with no button (see Approving a proposal on the register).

The products are those on the assistant's list: it cannot target a category without naming the products one by one, and you read the list.

What the screen shows. The title gives the number of products. It first reads the sales screen of the store (the get_sales_layout read tool: pages, buttons and free cells of each page, hidden buttons flagged), then files all the operations at once (the propose_sales_layout tool), in order: each operation sees the result of the previous ones, so it frees a cell before placing a button there. Lines and columns start at 1. A product with variants is placed as is: the register asks for the variant on tap. To read the screen, the assistant's key needs the Settings read access; to propose, the Edit the sales screen action, delivered switched off and switched on on request (see “Action subject to activation” above and “Allow the assistant to propose” below). Without either, the assistant does not see the tool and can only describe the screen. Ask for example: “Which buttons are on the Drinks page?” (read only), then “Add the beef tartare to the Mains page.”

Hidden buttons. A button whose product is archived or unavailable in this store stays in the screen but is not shown on the register. The assistant never removes them on its own: the proposal flags them with the note “Buttons left in place, hidden on the register: “Limonade artisanale” (page “Accueil”, row 3, column 1).” It may move, resize or remove one (“the hidden “Limonade artisanale” button (archived product)”). Adding an unavailable product is possible, with the warning ““Tartare de bœuf” is unavailable in this store: its button stays hidden until it is back on sale.”

When the screen is displayed nowhere. If the targeted screen is neither the default screen nor assigned to a register, the warning says: “No register shows this screen yet: the change will only appear once the screen is made the default or assigned to a register.” It blocks nothing.

The 64-operation limit. A proposal holds at most 64 operations (additions, moves, resizes and removals together). For more, the assistant prepares several proposals, which you approve one after the other.

One store at a time. The sales screen belongs to one store: a proposal never targets another store's screen, nor several stores. For two stores, the assistant files two proposals.

Who approves, and where. A manager decides in the back office only, with the Apply to the sales screen button. On the register and the floor phone, the proposal is listed and readable in full with the Back office badge and the sentence “Open the back office to decide.”, with no button (see Approve a proposal on the register).

Out of date. The proposal becomes out of date, and nothing is applied, if the screen's pages have changed since it was filed, even through another manager: “The sales screen has changed since the proposal.” (with the screen's name when the store has several). Other causes: “The sales screen no longer exists.”, “… is no longer a register screen.”, “… has been archived since the proposal.” and “The … category no longer exists.” Ask your assistant again: the new proposal starts from the screen as it is. A newer proposal on the same screen replaces the previous one.

Going back. Once applied, the change is undone like any other change to the sales screen: Sales screen › Restore previous version. Registers receive the restored screen at their next sync.

Expected result: the state becomes Applied, with the banner “Sales screen updated”, the Open the sales screen link and the sentence “Registers receive the new screen at their next sync.” The screen is identical to the one you would have saved by hand; the screen's history and the audit log keep a trace of the proposal.

Importing products ​

Action subject to activation. Vendora opens it separately from the assistant's other actions and it is not yet open for every account. As long as it is not open on your server, it does not appear in Settings › AI assistant and your assistant cannot propose it: contact Vendora to find out whether it is open for you.

You say “add these twelve products to my catalog and update the dairy prices”: the assistant files an Import products proposal, at high risk. It gathers 1 to 100 products: new products and updates to existing ones (name, category, barcode, selling price incl. VAT, purchase cost, stock tracking). Nothing exists and nothing changes until a manager approves, and it is all or nothing: if a single product changes before approval, nothing is applied.

What the screen shows.

  • The title and summary: “Import products · 12 products”, then “3 new products and 9 updated (Épicerie Lumière (démo))”, and the scope (“Épicerie Lumière (démo) · EUR” or “All stores · EUR”).
  • The table: one row per product (Product, Action “New” or “Updated”, Price before, Price incl. VAT, VAT, Other changes). A row to watch carries a reason: “Unusual rate for France”, “VAT differs from the category” or “Large change (+35%)”.
  • The notes, which start with the split of the new products' rates (“New products: 2 at the rate “TVA réduite 5,5 % — 5.5%”, 1 at the rate “TVA normale 20 % — 20%”.”), then remind you: “The VAT of existing products does not change.”, “Sales already recorded keep their prices and VAT.” and “All or nothing: if a single product changes before approval, nothing is applied.” A store that keeps its own price for an updated product is named: “The store “Lumière Tunis” keeps its own price for “Brie de Meaux” (12,50 €): the new price does not apply there.”

VAT: always named, never guessed.

  • New products. The assistant must name the rate of every new product. If it names none, the request is refused and the assistant explains why: you do not pick a rate at approval, and Vendora never takes a “default” rate on your behalf. A 0% rate is refused, as is a rate missing from your business or deactivated.
  • Existing products. Their VAT is never changed by an import: the assistant can restate the current rate, not another one. To correct it, do it by hand on the product's page.
  • A Tunisian store. Only the rates 7%, 13% and 19% are accepted for new products; any other rate is refused and the assistant is reminded of these three rates.
  • Another country (France, Morocco…): a rate that is unusual for that country is not refused but flagged (“1 new product has an unusual rate for France.”), as is a rate different from the one most products in the category use.

Price changes. When the price of an updated product changes by more than 30%, a warning at the top of the proposal says so (“1 price changes by more than 30%.”) and the row carries the reason “Large change (+35%)”. It is not a refusal: read the table before ticking the box.

The confirmation box. A single box that names every VAT rate of the new products and says how many products each covers: “I confirm the import of 12 products: 3 new (2 at the rate “TVA réduite 5,5 % — 5.5%”, 1 at the rate “TVA normale 20 % — 20%”) and 9 updated.” If there are flags, the sentence ends with “To check: 2 prices changed by more than 30%, 1 unusual rate for France…”. The Apply the import (12 products) button stays inactive until the box is ticked.

What the assistant refuses, explaining why (no proposal is then filed):

  • a new product without a name or a price; a price of 0 or one that is too high; an invalid or already used name; a barcode that is invalid, reserved (starting with 20 to 29) or already carried by another product;
  • an update that changes nothing;
  • a category that is not found or is ambiguous, or whose name contains “>”: an import only files products in existing categories and creates none; file that product by hand;
  • a product with variants (it is edited in the back office);
  • with a store specified, the price of a business-wide product: it is changed by a price proposal for that store, not by an import;
  • several currencies in the same proposal (one import per currency is needed), or stores that do not share the same currency and country when the store for the new products is not specified.

One store at a time. New products belong to the named store, or apply to all stores if the assistant names none and your stores share the same currency and country. An assistant whose access is limited to some stores must always name the store and only changes products that belong to that store.

Who approves, and where. A manager decides in the back office only, with the Apply the import button. On the register and the floor phone, the proposal is listed and readable with the Back office badge, without a button (see Approve an assistant proposal on the register). A manager who is not in charge of the store concerned cannot decide it: it stays pending. New products count towards your plan's product limit; if it is reached, approval is refused and the proposal stays pending.

Expected result: the state becomes Applied, with the “Import applied” banner, the View the products link and the sentence “Registers receive the products at their next sync.” The import is the same as a file import (see Import your catalog from a CSV file) and the audit log keeps a trace of it. There is no button to undo it: correct a price or withdraw a product from sale, by hand or with a new proposal. A sale already recorded keeps the product's rate at the time of the sale, even if you correct it afterwards.

Out of date. The proposal becomes out of date, and nothing is imported, if a product it targets has changed since it was filed (including a price, a name or a barcode), was withdrawn from sale or gained variants, if its VAT has changed (“The VAT of “Brie de Meaux” has changed (5,5 % → 20 %).”), if a rate is no longer active (“The rate “TVA réduite 5,5 % — 5.5%” is no longer active.”), if a category has disappeared, if a product with the same name or an identical barcode has appeared elsewhere, if a store has its own price or was archived, or if a store's country has changed. Vendora quotes the first cause, followed by “(and 3 other lines)” when there are several. When it cannot be precise: “The catalog has changed since the proposal.” Ask your assistant again: the new proposal starts from the catalog as it is.

Correcting stock after a count, recording a loss ​

Action subject to activation. Vendora opens it separately from the assistant's other actions and it is not yet open for every account. As long as it is not open on your server, it does not appear in Settings › AI assistant and your assistant cannot propose it: contact Vendora to find out whether it is open for you.

You say “I counted 18 bottles of still water and 4 packs of milk” or “two bottles of rosé broke this morning”: the assistant files a Correct stock proposal (at high risk) or a Stock loss proposal (at medium risk). Each one gathers 1 to 100 products of a single store. Stock is never overwritten: each line becomes a signed movement, written in your name and dated at your approval. Nothing moves until a manager approves, and it is all or nothing.

Correcting stock after a count.

  • What the assistant provides: the counted quantity, nothing else. The difference is calculated by Vendora, never by the assistant.
  • What the screen shows: the title “Correct stock · {product}” (or “· 3 products”), then the What will be recorded in the stock log table, one row per product: Product, Theoretical stock, Counted, Difference and Value of difference (“—” when the product has no average cost). The difference is signed: “+3” when the system is short, “−6” when it holds too much.
  • The difference is recalculated at approval. It is the count minus the stock Vendora believed it had when the proposal was made, including sales synced since (an offline register may have sent them later). Sales made after the proposal do not change the difference: they lowered real and theoretical stock together. A line whose recalculated difference is zero writes nothing (“Stock corrected: 2 adjustments recorded in the stock log, 1 with no difference.”).
  • A large difference. When a line differs by at least 2 units and by more than 20% of theoretical stock (or theoretical stock is zero or negative), a warning appears at the top (“Large difference on 2 products (over 20% of theoretical stock): check the count before validating.”), the line shows “Large difference” and a box is required: “I confirm the large differences on 2 products”. A large difference can hide a theft: the box proves you have read it.

Recording a loss.

  • What the screen shows: the title “Stock loss · {product}”, then the table with Product, Stock at proposal time, Quantity removed and Value at average cost.
  • You choose the reason: Breakage, Expiry, Damaged, Theft or Other. The assistant can pre-fill Breakage, Expiry, Damaged or Other (“pre-filled by the assistant, editable”). It never picks “Theft”: if the reason is a theft, it gives none and the choice is yours. With no reason given, nothing is pre-selected and the button stays inactive until you choose.
  • What is written: a Theft movement if you choose “Theft”, a Loss movement (with the reason you chose) otherwise. The assistant's justification is never copied into the stock log.

Who approves, and where. A manager of the store approves in the back office or on a register of the store, always with their PIN. On the register, the reason suggested by the assistant is preselected and can be changed (never “Theft”), and a box must be ticked before the PIN when the difference is large. On the waiter phone, the proposal can be read without a button, with the sentence “Approve it on a register or in the back office, not on a floor phone.” (see Approve a proposal on the register). A manager who is not in charge of the store concerned cannot decide it: it stays pending.

Step by step to approve.

  1. Open the proposal in Proposals and read the table (and the note that recalls how the difference is calculated).
  2. For a loss: choose the reason. For a large difference: tick the box.
  3. Click the Validate and correct stock button (or Validate and remove from stock), marked with a padlock.
  4. In the Manager PIN window, enter your PIN: the reason and the box go with it. A wrong PIN leaves the window open (“Invalid PIN: 2 attempts left before the one-minute lock.”); without a PIN, nothing is applied.

Expected result: the state becomes Applied, with “Stock corrected: … adjustments recorded in the stock log” or “Loss recorded in the stock log (1 movement).”, and the View the stock log link. Registers receive the new stock at their next sync. Each movement carries the mention prepared by the assistant · validated by {your name} (see Understand the stock log). There is no button to undo: a mistake is corrected in the stock log with a reversing movement.

What the assistant refuses, explaining why (no proposal is then filed): a store that is not named (stock is corrected store by store); a product withdrawn from sale, not stock-tracked or with variants (the variant must be named); a negative quantity, a zero quantity for a loss, more than 100,000 units, or a fractional quantity for a product sold by the piece (1.5 kg is accepted, 1.5 pieces is not); a count equal to theoretical stock (“nothing to correct”); a product with a count in progress (“stock will be corrected when it's validated”).

Out of date. The proposal becomes out of date, and nothing is written, when stock has moved since it was filed:

  • Correction after a count: a movement other than a sale or a customer return was recorded for the product (receipt of goods, transfer, loss, theft, adjustment, opening stock, reversing movement), or a stock count covering the product was started or validated since (“Stock for “Still water 1.5 L” has moved since the proposal (delivery, loss, adjustment, transfer or count): the difference is no longer reliable.”). Sales and customer returns do not make it out of date.
  • Loss: a manual movement was recorded for the product (loss, theft, adjustment, opening stock, reversing movement), because the same breakage would be counted twice (“A loss, theft or adjustment has already been recorded for “Still water 1.5 L” since the proposal: it would be counted twice.”). A receipt, a transfer or a sale does not make it out of date.
  • In both cases, a product withdrawn from sale, no longer stock-tracked, given variants or whose unit changed (“Still water 1.5 L” is no longer stock-tracked, was withdrawn from sale or changed unit.”), or a count started for the product (“A stock count has been started for “Still water 1.5 L”: validate it first.”).

Vendora quotes the first cause, followed by “(and 3 other lines)” when there are several. Ask your assistant for the proposal again: it starts from stock as it is.

What the assistant refuses (no proposal is filed then): an order still in draft, already received or cancelled; a supplier with several orders to receive (it asks which one); any mention of cost, date, lot or note.

Out of date. The proposal becomes out of date, and nothing is written, when the order has been received, closed or cancelled since, when another receipt of the order has been recorded since the proposal (it would be counted twice, even if made on an offline register), or when a product now requires a lot and an expiry date. By default, an assistant reads your figures and proposes nothing. The owner or an administrator sets what it may propose:

  1. Settings › AI assistant, What the assistant can propose block: tick the families of actions allowed for your business.
  2. When connecting an assistant or creating a key, tick the families granted to that assistant: none is ticked in advance, and a family allowed here is granted to nobody until you tick it.
  3. Also tick the read access the family needs (“Purchases” for a purchase order, “Catalog” for a price, “Anti-fraud” for an alert).

Details: Connect your AI assistant and Connect an AI assistant in one click. Even when allowed, the assistant sends, pays and publishes nothing. The emergency stop prevents it from filing new proposals; you can still decide on those waiting.

If it does not work

The Proposals menu does not appear

No proposal has been filed yet, or your account is Cashier. Also check that a family of actions was ticked when the assistant was connected.

The Proposal family selector does not appear

It only appears when the displayed list contains at least two families. Change the state filter (for example All).

“The Suppliers module is no longer in your plan: approval is not possible.”

Purchase orders need the Suppliers module. Check Settings › Subscription; you can still decline.

“You do not have the role to approve this proposal.”

Your account is not allowed to decide here. Ask a manager, an administrator or the owner.

“No connection: nothing was approved. Try again.”

The approval did not go through. Restore the connection, then click again: nothing was applied in the meantime.

“The confirmation box is missing: nothing was applied.”

Tick the confirmation box (or fill in the requested field), then approve again.

The approve button stays inactive

A required field is empty (the reason of an alert, or the VAT rate of a new product) or the confirmation box for a change above 30% (or an option removal) is not ticked. If the button is missing, the details say why your account cannot decide.
